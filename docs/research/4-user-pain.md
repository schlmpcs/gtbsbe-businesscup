# 4. User pain: US adults 25–45 and big money decisions (research as of 2026-11-14)

**Method note.** These figures come from web search result summaries. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session: federalreserve.gov, newyorkfed.org, edmunds.com and care.com all failed DNS. So each number below is checked against the search snippet of the primary source, or against two or more outlets that agree.
- **VERIFIED-PRIMARY:** the primary source's own text appeared in the search results.
- **SECONDARY:** the number comes only from news coverage of the primary source.
- **UNVERIFIED:** the number could not be confirmed.

Before putting any number on a slide, open the URL and check it.

## B. Financial stress and anxiety

- **APA Stress in America 2025** (The Harris Poll, 3,199 adults). Top stressors: the economy, cited by **75%**, and money concerns, cited by **66%**. https://www.apa.org/pubs/reports/stress-in-america/2025
  - SECONDARY: the snippet says the **2026** report has **71%** saying money is a significant source of stress (63% in 2023), and **77%** citing cost of living. https://www.apa.org/pubs/reports/stress-in-america/2026/index
  - I found **no 2025/2026 breakdown by age.** An older APA wave (year unconfirmed, UNVERIFIED) gave: 69% of ages 26–39 and 70% of ages 40–55 cited financial concerns.
- **Gallup Economy & Personal Finance, April 2026** (1,001 adults, April 1–15). https://news.gallup.com/poll/708905/affordability-dominates-americans-financial-worries.aspx (SECONDARY, via CBS, Axios and Washington Times)
  - A record **55%** say their financial situation is getting worse. This was 53% in 2025 and 47% in 2024.
  - **62%** worry about not having enough for retirement.
  - **60%** worry about paying medical costs after a serious illness or accident.
  - **41%** worry about paying normal monthly bills.
  - **31%** name inflation and rising prices as their top money problem.
- **CNBC|SurveyMonkey Your Money survey, April 2026.** https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/cnbc-money-survey-affordability-april-2026/ (SECONDARY)
  - **50%** are more stressed financially than a year ago.
  - How people describe their situation: 41% "managing", **21% "struggling"**, **9% "falling behind"**.
  - **Millennials are the most likely generation to say they are falling behind: 15%**, vs 8% of Gen Z and 9% of Gen X.
- **Northwestern Mutual 2026 Planning & Progress Study** (The Harris Poll, 4,375 adults, January 5–21, 2026).
  - **50%** feel financially secure (44% in 2025). https://news.northwesternmutual.com/planning-and-progress-study-2026
  - Retirement "magic number": **$1.46M** (up from $1.26M in 2025). https://news.northwesternmutual.com/2026-04-01-Americans-Believe-They-Will-Need-1-46-Million-to-Retire-Comfortably,-Up-More-Than-15-Since-Last-Year,-According-to-Northwestern-Mutual-2026-Planning-Progress-Study
  - Among people using or considering prediction markets, sports betting or crypto: **73%** say they do it because they **feel financially behind** (Gen Z 80%, Millennials 75%). Caution: this applies only to that subgroup, not to all adults. https://news.northwesternmutual.com/2026-03-09-Americans-Finances-are-Improving-But-Some-Still-Feel-Behind-and-are-Turning-to-Prediction-Markets,-Sports-Betting-and-Crypto-to-Catch-Up,-According-to-Northwestern-Mutuals-2026-Planning-Progress-Study
  - Delayed milestones (August 2026 wave, SECONDARY):
    - **71% of Gen Z and 60% of Millennials** believe they may never afford at least one of life's financial milestones.
    - **72% of Gen Z and 56% of Millennials** postponed at least one major life event for financial reasons.
    - Source: https://news.northwesternmutual.com/2026-08-03-Financial-Pressures-Are-Pushing-Younger-Americans-to-Delay-and-Sometimes-Abandon-Lifes-Biggest-Milestones
  - Advisor effect: **71%** of people with a financial advisor feel financially secure. The 10% figure for people without one is ambiguous in coverage, so it is UNVERIFIED. https://247wallst.com/personal-finance/2026/05/02/71-of-americans-with-financial-advisors-feel-secure-only-10-without-one-do/
- **Bankrate Emergency Savings Report, February 2026** (SSRS/YouGov). https://www.bankrate.com/banking/savings/emergency-savings-survey
  - **29%** have more credit card debt than emergency savings. Millennials are the highest generation at **35%**, then Gen X at 33%.
  - **58%** say their emergency savings did not grow in the past year.

## C. What big decisions cost

### Cars
- **Edmunds, Q3 2026** (released October 2026). https://www.edmunds.com/press/new-car-financing-records-pile-up-in-q3-as-buyers-borrow-more-and-stretch-loans-longer-according-to-edmunds.html (SECONDARY, via trade press: GM Authority, Ford Authority, Click2Houston)
  - Average new-vehicle monthly payment: **$787**, a record (Q2 2026: $777; Q3 2025: $756).
  - Loans of **84 months or longer**: **25.5%** of financed new-vehicle purchases, a record (Q3 2025: 21.8%).
  - Payments of **$1,000 a month or more**: **21.2%** of new-vehicle financing (trade press; not seen in the release text).
  - Average amount financed: **$44,664**. Average APR: about **7.0%**.
- **Edmunds negative equity, Q3 2026** (SECONDARY, CBT News and others).
  - **29.1%** of trade-ins toward new-vehicle purchases were underwater.
  - Average negative equity: **$7,107**.
  - Buyers who rolled that negative equity into the new loan: average financed **$57,235**, average payment **$952** a month.
  - Do not mix with Q3 2025 figures (28.1%, $6,905): https://www.edmunds.com/press/underwater-and-sinking-deeper-the-average-amount-owed-on-upside-down-auto-loans-climbed-to-an-all-time-high-of-6905-according-to-edmunds.html
- **Experian State of the Automotive Finance Market, Q2 2026.** https://www.experian.com/blogs/insights/auto-from-hybrids-to-refinancing-consumers-are-finding-new-roads-to-vehicle-affordability/
  - New vehicles: average payment **$765** a month, average loan **$43,610**, average rate **6.35%**.
  - Used vehicles: average payment **$542** a month, average loan $27,852.
  - Refinancing: an average refinance saves **$83** a month (7.97% vs an original 10.40%).
  - Average loan term: not found (the report is gated).

### Childcare
- **Care.com 2026 Cost of Care Report.** https://www.care.com/c/how-much-does-child-care-cost
  - Average weekly cost: daycare **$332**, nanny **$870**, babysitter $175.
  - Parents say they spend on average **20% of household income** on childcare. The HHS affordability benchmark is 7%.
- **Child Care Aware of America, *Child Care in America: 2025 Price & Supply*.** https://info.childcareaware.org/price-and-supply-2025
  - National average annual price: **$13,184**.
  - That is about **10%** of median income for married couples with children and **33%** for single parents.
  - Infant center care, full time, by state: $6,492 (Mississippi) to $27,067 (Massachusetts).

### Rent
- **Census ACS 2024 1-year estimates** (released September 2025). https://census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/acs-1-year-estimates.html
  - Median gross rent: **$1,487 a month** (+2.7% vs 2023).
  - Median share of income spent on rent: **31%**.
- **Harvard JCHS, *State of the Nation's Housing 2026*** (released 2026-06-17; data for 2024). (JCHS URL not confirmed; coverage: https://www.gsd.harvard.edu/2026/06/state-of-the-nations-housing-2026-finds-affordability-challenges/)
  - A record **22.7 million** renter households were cost-burdened, about **half** of all renters.
  - **12.1 million** were severely burdened, meaning they paid 50% or more of income on housing.

### Credit cards
- **NY Fed Household Debt and Credit Report, Q3 2026** (released 2026-11-10). https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110 (PDF: https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/interactives/householdcredit/data/pdf/hhdc_2026q3.pdf)
  - Credit card balances: **$1.285 trillion**, a record (+$22B in the quarter).
  - Total household debt: about **$19.1 trillion**.
  - Flow of card balances into serious (90+ day) delinquency: **6.87%**.
  - Borrowers **under 30** had the highest rate of newly becoming 90+ days overdue ([Axios](https://axios.com/2026/11/10/credit-card-debt-record-2026-delinquency-income-gap)).
- **Fed G.19, November 6, 2026 release** (Q3 2026). https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/20261106/g19.pdf (FRED series TERMCBCCINTNS)
  - Average APR on accounts assessed interest: **22.36%**.
  - Average APR on all accounts: **21.19%**.
- **TransUnion** (SECONDARY): average card balance per consumer **$6,678**.

### Buy now, pay later (BNPL)
- **LendingTree BNPL survey, 2026** (2,060 consumers, March 17–23, 2026). https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/buy-now-pay-later-loan-statistics/
  - **47%** of BNPL users paid late in the past year. This was 41% in 2025 and 34% in 2024.

### Student loans
- **NY Fed Q3 2026** (SECONDARY, via The College Investor). https://thecollegeinvestor.com/91905/student-loan-delinquency-hits-10-9-in-new-york-fed-q3-2026/
  - **10.9%** of student loan balances were 90+ days delinquent or in default (9.4% a year earlier). Total balances: $1.656 trillion.
  - Q1 2026: about **2.6 million** borrowers defaulted ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/york-fed-2-6-million-185625419.html)).
- **NY Fed Liberty Street Economics** (May 2026). https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/05/federal-student-loan-defaults-return-after-pa
  - Credit scores of defaulted borrowers fell **91 points**, from 567 to 476, between 2024 Q3 and 2025 Q4.

## D. Regret and lack of planning

- **Bankrate Financial Regrets Survey 2025** (July 9–11, 2025). https://www.bankrate.com/press-releases/nearly-3-in-4-americans-have-a-financial-regret-in-2025-retirement-and-credit-card-debt-top-regrets/
  - **74%** have a financial regret.
  - Top regrets: not saving for retirement early enough (**22%**) and too much credit card debt (15%).
  - Millennials' top regrets are tied at **17%** each: too much credit card debt, and not saving enough for emergencies.
- **Bankrate homeowner regret** (April 2025, 1,363 homeowners). https://www.bankrate.com/press-releases/nearly-half-of-homeowners-have-at-least-one-regret-about-the-purchase-of-their-current-home/
  - **45%** have at least one regret about buying their current home.
  - Of those, **42%** say maintenance and hidden costs were higher than expected.
- **J.D. Power, June 2025** (SECONDARY): first-time buyers' top regret is not getting better information about the **total cost of homeownership**. https://rdcnewsadvice.wpengine.com/news/trends/first-time-homebuyer-regret-survey/
- **Car regret, LendingTree** (survey of about 2,000 car owners; survey date not confirmed).
  - About **39–40%** regret their car purchase, rising to 47% of those who bought in the past year.
  - Source: https://lendingtree.com/auto/car-regrets-survey
  - Status: UNVERIFIED for 2025–2026.
- **Budgeting:**
  - Ramsey Solutions 2026 (SECONDARY): **47%** create a monthly budget (39% in 2021).
  - YouGov 2026: **53%** have set a budget for 2026, and 38% have not. https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/54197-us-consumer-spending-and-budgeting-trends-in-2026
  - Verasight, May 2026: only **18.4%** keep a detailed monthly budget and track most of their money. https://data.verasight.io/culture/most-americans-track-their-finances-loosely-if-at-all
  - Debt.com 2026: 85% say they budget. This is a loose question wording, so the number is inflated.
- **Budgeting apps:**
  - PYMNTS (May 2025, 2,040 consumers): **14%** use a budgeting app daily. https://www.pymnts.com/mobile/2025/daily-budget-app-adoption-stalls-at-14-percent/
  - Debt.com 2026, via Walnut Invest (SECONDARY): 21.7% currently use budgeting apps, and **38.4%** want to try them.
- **Written financial plan:**
  - Schwab Modern Wealth Survey 2025 (Logica Research, 2,000 adults aged 21–75): about **33%** have a written plan. This is UNVERIFIED; it comes from Boldin, and the 2024 survey said 36%.
  - Gen Z: 39%. https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/wealth-is-more-than-money
- **People underestimate spending:**
  - C+R Research: people guessed **$86** a month on subscriptions. When they itemized, the actual figure was **$219**, a gap of $133. Survey of 1,000 consumers.
  - The underlying study is from **2022, not 2024–2026**. https://www.crresearch.com/blog/true-cost-of-subscriptions
  - If used, label it "C+R Research, 2022".
- **Paycheck to paycheck** (Debt.com): 48% in 2026, down from 69% in 2025. SECONDARY, and volatile across sources.

## E. Willingness to pay, and AI for money advice

- **Gallup / Edward Jones, August 2026.**
  - About **1 in 5** people who sought financial advice in the past year used AI.
  - **Under one-third** have confidence in advice from AI, vs **79%** for human advisors.
  - Confidence in AI is highest among Millennials (**36%**) and Gen Z (32%). Both groups still prefer human professionals.
  - Source: https://abcnews.com/Technology/wireStory/us-adults-ai-financial-guidance-trust-gallup-poll-135452999
- **TD Bank 2026 survey** (2,500 consumers, SECONDARY).
  - **55%** have asked an LLM for financial advice, up from 10% the year before.
  - Gen Z **77%** and Millennials **72%** use AI for financial decisions.
  - Only **18%** would trust AI to act autonomously.
  - Source: https://www.americanbanker.com/news/more-americans-asking-ai-for-financial-advice-td-survey
- **Willingness to pay:** I found **no credible 2025–2026 survey** that measures willingness to pay for a personal finance app by generation (UNVERIFIED gap). Proxies:
  - Northwestern Mutual 2026: **24% of Gen Z and 25% of Millennials** saw a financial advisor for the first time in the past year. This is paid human guidance, so it shows demand for guidance.
  - Employer-sponsored advice (BenefitsPro / HR Executive, October 2025): Millennials and Gen Z would pay **$25–$99 an hour**, median **$48 an hour**. https://hrexecutive.com/millennials-gen-z-willing-to-pay-for-employer-sponsored-investment-advice/
  - Pinwheel whitepaper (vendor): 53% would pay for a premium banking tier with subscription management. This is low quality evidence.
  - Pitch logic: a human advisor costs about $48 an hour, and AI is trusted only about a third as much as humans. So the product should keep the math deterministic and auditable and use AI only for language, which addresses the trust gap.
